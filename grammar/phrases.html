English Phrases

Learn what phrases are, the major types of phrases, and how to use them correctly with simple examples.

What is a Phrase?

A phrase is a group of words that works together as a unit but does not normally contain a complete subject-verb combination expressing a complete thought.

Quick idea: “in the garden” is a phrase. It adds information but cannot normally stand alone as a complete sentence.

Noun Phrase

A noun phrase is a group of words that functions as a noun. It may contain a noun along with determiners, adjectives and other modifiers.

The little boy is playing.
I bought a new English book.

Prepositional Phrase

A prepositional phrase usually begins with a preposition and includes its object. It can provide information about place, time, direction and more.

The keys are on the table.
We met after the class.

Verb Phrase

A verb phrase consists of a main verb and, when present, one or more helping verbs.

She is studying English.
They have been waiting for an hour.

Infinitive Phrase

An infinitive phrase commonly begins with to + base verb and can function in different ways in a sentence.

She wants to learn English.
To help others is a good habit.

Gerund Phrase

A gerund is an -ing form that functions as a noun. A gerund phrase includes the gerund and its modifiers or complements.

Reading English books improves vocabulary.
He enjoys playing cricket.

Participial Phrase

A participial phrase begins with a present or past participle and acts as an adjective, describing a noun or pronoun.

Walking along the road, the boy saw a dog.
The girl, wearing a blue dress, is my sister.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing a phrase with a clause.
  • Assuming every group of words with an -ing form is a gerund phrase; its function in the sentence matters.
  • Misplacing a participial phrase so that it appears to describe the wrong noun.
  • Using a phrase as a complete sentence when it does not express a complete thought.
