ENGLISH GRAMMAR • PRONOUNS

Pronouns made simple.

Learn the different types of English pronouns with simple rules, clear examples, common mistakes and practice questions.

What is a Pronoun?

A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun. Pronouns help us avoid repeating the same noun again and again.

Quick idea: Instead of saying “Riya said Riya would complete Riya's work,” we can say: “Riya said she would complete her work.”

Types of Pronouns

Type Common Pronouns Example
Personal I, you, he, she, it, we, they She is my friend.
Object me, you, him, her, it, us, them She called me.
Possessive mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs This book is mine.
Reflexive myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves He hurt himself.
Demonstrative this, that, these, those These are my books.
Interrogative who, whom, whose, which, what Who is at the door?
Relative who, whom, whose, which, that The man who called is my uncle.
Indefinite someone, anyone, everyone, nobody, something Someone is waiting outside.

Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns refer to particular people, animals or things. They can act as subjects or objects.

Subject Pronouns

I, you, he, she, it, we, they

She teaches English.
They are playing cricket.

Object Pronouns

me, you, him, her, it, us, them

Rahul called me.
I helped them.

Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns show ownership or possession.

This pen is mine.
That house is theirs.
Remember: Possessive pronouns such as mine, yours, his, hers, ours and theirs can stand alone. Do not use a noun directly after them.

Reflexive Pronouns

Reflexive pronouns are used when the subject and object refer to the same person or thing.

I hurt myself.
She introduced herself.
They enjoyed themselves.

Demonstrative Pronouns

Demonstrative pronouns point to particular people or things.

This is my phone.
That is your bag.
These are my shoes.
Those are expensive cars.

Interrogative Pronouns

Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions.

Who is your teacher?
Whose book is this?
Which is your favourite?

Common Mistakes

✕ Me and Rahul went to school.
✓ Rahul and I went to school.
✕ This book is her.
✓ This book is hers.
✕ He did it by hisself.
✓ He did it by himself.
✕ Myself Rahul.
✓ I am Rahul.

How to Learn Pronouns

  • First learn subject and object pronouns.
  • Practice possessive forms separately.
  • Remember reflexive pronouns carefully.
  • Learn pronouns through complete sentences.
  • Always check whether the pronoun agrees with its noun.
