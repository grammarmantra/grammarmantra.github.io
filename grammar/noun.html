English Nouns

Learn what nouns are, the major types of nouns, and how to use them correctly with simple rules and examples.

What is a Noun?

A noun is a word used to name a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Quick idea: Words such as teacher, Delhi, dog, book and honesty are nouns because they name people, places, animals, things or ideas.

Types of Nouns

TypeMeaningExamples
Common NounA general name for a person, place, animal or thing.boy, city, river, book
Proper NounA specific name of a person, place or thing.Rahul, India, Delhi
Collective NounA word that names a group.team, crowd, committee
Abstract NounA name for an idea, quality, feeling or state.honesty, beauty, happiness
Material NounA substance or material from which things are made.gold, water, iron
Countable NounA noun that can be counted.book, apple, student
Uncountable NounA noun that is generally not counted individually.water, advice, information

Common and Proper Nouns

Common Noun

A common noun gives a general name and is normally not capitalized unless it begins a sentence.

The boy is reading a book.

Proper Noun

A proper noun gives a specific name and normally begins with a capital letter.

Rahul lives in Delhi.

Collective Nouns

A collective noun refers to a group considered as a unit.

A team of players won the match.
The crowd was excited.

Abstract Nouns

Abstract nouns name things that cannot normally be seen or touched, such as qualities, feelings and ideas.

Honesty is a great quality.
Happiness is important in life.

Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Countable nouns can have singular and plural forms.

one book → two books

Uncountable nouns are generally treated as mass nouns and do not normally take a plural form.

some water ✓   |   two waters ✗
some information ✓   |   informations ✗

Singular and Plural Nouns

A singular noun refers to one person, place, animal or thing. A plural noun refers to more than one.

one student → two students
one class → two classes

Many nouns form their plural by adding -s or -es, but some nouns have irregular plural forms.

child → children   |   man → men   |   foot → feet

Possessive Nouns

A possessive noun shows ownership or relationship. For a singular noun, an apostrophe and s are commonly used.

This is Riya's book.

For many regular plural nouns ending in -s, an apostrophe is generally added after the s.

The students' books are on the table.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a capital letter for every common noun.
  • Adding plural -s to uncountable nouns such as information and advice.
  • Confusing a general name with a specific name.
  • Forgetting irregular plural forms such as child → children.
