English Conjunctions

Learn how conjunctions connect words, phrases and clauses, with coordinating, subordinating and correlative conjunctions.

What is a Conjunction?

A conjunction is a word used to join words, phrases or clauses. Conjunctions help connect ideas and make sentences smoother.

Quick idea: In “Riya and Priya are friends,” the word and joins two words. In “I stayed home because it was raining,” it connects two clauses.

Types of Conjunctions

TypePurposeCommon Examples
CoordinatingJoins words or clauses of equal importancefor, and, nor, but, or, yet, so
SubordinatingJoins a dependent clause to a main clausebecause, although, if, when, while, since

Coordinating Conjunctions

The seven common coordinating conjunctions are remembered by the word FANBOYS:

For • And • Nor • But • Or • Yet • So

And — Addition

Riya and Priya are studying.

But — Contrast

He is poor but honest.

Or — Choice

Would you like tea or coffee?

So — Result

It was raining, so we stayed home.

Yet — Unexpected Contrast

He is young, yet very wise.

Subordinating Conjunctions

Subordinating conjunctions introduce a dependent clause and show relationships such as reason, time, condition, contrast and purpose.

Reason

I stayed home because I was tired.

Condition

If you work hard, you will succeed.

Time

Call me when you arrive.

Contrast

Although he was tired, he continued working.

Common Subordinating Conjunctions

because, although, though, if, unless, when, while, before, after, since, until, as, whereas

Correlative Conjunctions

Correlative conjunctions work in pairs. They connect words, phrases or clauses that have the same grammatical role.

Either Riya or Priya will come.
Neither the teacher nor the students were ready.
Both English and Hindi are taught here.
She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

Conjunctions & Punctuation

When two independent clauses are joined by a coordinating conjunction, a comma is commonly used before the conjunction.

I wanted to go, but I was busy.

When a dependent clause comes before the main clause, a comma is commonly used after the dependent clause.

Because it was raining, we stayed home.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a conjunction that does not express the intended relationship between ideas.
  • Using “although” and “but” together in the same clause structure: “Although he was tired, but he worked” is incorrect.
  • Forgetting the correct pair in correlative conjunctions.
  • Using “and” when the sentence actually needs a contrast or condition.
