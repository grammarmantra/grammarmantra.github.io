Direct & Indirect Speech

Learn how to report what someone said using direct and indirect speech, with clear rules, tense changes and practical examples.

What is Direct and Indirect Speech?

Direct Speech gives the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks. Indirect Speech reports the speaker's meaning without repeating the exact words.

Quick idea: Direct Speech uses the speaker's exact words. Indirect Speech changes the sentence into a reported form.

Direct vs Indirect Speech

TypeExampleKey Feature
Direct SpeechRiya said, “I am tired.”Exact words are quoted.
Indirect SpeechRiya said that she was tired.The message is reported.

Basic Rules

1. Remove the quotation marks

When changing direct speech into indirect speech, the quotation marks are normally removed.

He said, “I am busy.” → He said that he was busy.

2. Change the pronoun when necessary

Pronouns are changed according to the speaker, listener and context.

She said, “I am ready.” → She said that she was ready.

3. Change the tense when required

When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense often moves one step back.

4. Change words showing time or place

Words may change according to the context.

“I will come tomorrow.” → He said that he would come the next day.
“I am busy now.” → He said that he was busy then.

Statements

For ordinary statements, that is commonly used to introduce the reported clause.

Direct: She said, “I like English.”
Indirect: She said that she liked English.

Questions

In reported questions, the sentence follows statement word order. For yes/no questions, if or whether is commonly used.

Direct: He said, “Are you ready?”
Indirect: He asked if I was ready.
Direct: She said, “Where do you live?”
Indirect: She asked where I lived.

Commands, Requests and Advice

Commands and requests are commonly reported using verbs such as told, ordered, requested, advised and an infinitive structure.

Direct: The teacher said, “Open the book.”
Indirect: The teacher told the students to open the book.
Direct: He said, “Please help me.”
Indirect: He requested me to help him.

Common Mistakes

  • Keeping quotation marks in indirect speech.
  • Forgetting necessary pronoun changes.
  • Changing the tense incorrectly.
  • Using question word order in a reported question.
  • Changing time expressions without considering the context.
