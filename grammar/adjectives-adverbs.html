Adjectives & Adverbs

Learn how adjectives and adverbs describe words, how to identify them, and how to use them correctly with simple rules and examples.

Adjectives vs Adverbs

An adjective describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. An adverb commonly modifies a verb, adjective or another adverb.

Quick idea: “She is a careful driver” uses an adjective. “She drives carefully” uses an adverb.

The Basic Difference

WordWhat it modifiesExample
AdjectiveNoun or pronounShe is a quick learner.
AdverbVerb, adjective or another adverbShe learns quickly.

Adjectives

Adjectives give information about a noun or pronoun, such as quality, size, color, number or quantity.

Examples

It is a beautiful flower.
He bought a new car.
She has three books.

Common Types of Adjectives

  • Quality: beautiful, honest, clever
  • Quantity: much, little, enough
  • Number: one, two, first, several
  • Demonstrative: this, that, these, those
  • Possessive: my, your, his, her, their
  • Interrogative: which, what, whose

Adverbs

Adverbs add information about an action or description. They commonly tell us how, when, where or how often something happens.

Adverbs of Manner

They tell us how an action happens.

She sings beautifully.
He completed the work carefully.

Adverbs of Time

They tell us when something happens.

I will call you tomorrow.
She arrived yesterday.

Adverbs of Place

They tell us where something happens.

Come here.
The children are playing outside.

Adverbs of Frequency

They tell us how often something happens.

He always speaks politely.
She rarely eats fast food.

Forming Adverbs

Many adverbs are formed by adding -ly to an adjective, although this is not true for every adverb.

quick → quickly
careful → carefully
slow → slowly

Some common adverbs do not end in -ly, such as fast, hard, late and early.

Adjective and Adverb Comparison

Both adjectives and adverbs can have comparative and superlative forms, depending on the word.

He is a fast runner. → He runs fast.
She works hard. → She works harder than me.
He speaks more clearly than before.

Commonly Confused Words

AdjectiveAdverbExample
goodwellShe sings well.
quickquicklyHe ran quickly.
carefulcarefullyDrive carefully.
beautifulbeautifullyShe danced beautifully.

Common Mistakes

  • Using an adjective when an adverb is needed to describe a verb.
  • Assuming every adverb must end in -ly.
  • Confusing good and well.
  • Using “more” with an adjective that already has the correct comparative form, such as “more better”.
