English Clauses

Learn what clauses are, how independent and dependent clauses work, and the main types of dependent clauses with simple examples.

What is a Clause?

A clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb. A sentence may contain one clause or several clauses.

Quick idea: “She smiled” is a clause because it has a subject (she) and a verb (smiled). “Because she smiled” also has a subject and verb, but it does not express a complete thought by itself.

Main Types of Clauses

TypeMeaningExample
Independent ClauseCan stand alone as a complete sentence.She went home.
Dependent ClauseCannot normally stand alone and depends on a main clause.because she was tired

Independent Clause

An independent clause expresses a complete thought and can function as a complete sentence.

Riya studies English.
The children are playing.

Two independent clauses can be joined with a coordinating conjunction.

I was tired, but I finished my work.

Dependent Clause

A dependent clause has a subject and a verb, but it does not normally express a complete thought on its own. It needs an independent clause to complete the meaning.

because I was tired ✗
I went to bed because I was tired. ✓

Noun Clause

A noun clause functions like a noun in a sentence. It can act as a subject, object or complement.

What she said was true.
I know that he is honest.

Adjective Clause

An adjective clause, also called a relative clause, describes or gives more information about a noun or pronoun. It often begins with words such as who, whom, whose, which or that.

The girl who won the prize is my friend.
This is the book that I bought yesterday.

Adverb Clause

An adverb clause functions like an adverb. It can tell us about time, reason, condition, contrast, purpose and other relationships.

I stayed home because it was raining.
When the teacher arrived, the class became quiet.
If you work hard, you will succeed.

Clauses in Sentences

Simple Sentence

Contains one independent clause.

She reads every day.

Compound Sentence

Usually contains two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction.

She reads every day, and she writes every evening.

Complex Sentence

Contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.

Although she was tired, she continued studying.

Common Mistakes

  • Thinking every group of words is a clause. A clause needs a subject and a verb.
  • Using a dependent clause as a complete sentence.
  • Joining independent clauses incorrectly with a comma alone.
  • Confusing a phrase with a clause. A phrase does not contain both a subject and a finite verb in the same way a clause does.
