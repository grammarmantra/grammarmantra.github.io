ENGLISH GRAMMAR • MODALS

English Modals

Learn can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would and other modal verbs with simple rules, uses, structures and examples.

What are Modals?

Modal verbs are helping verbs used with a main verb to express ideas such as ability, possibility, permission, necessity, advice and prediction.

Quick idea: A modal is normally followed by the base form of a verb: can go, should study, must work.

Common Modal Verbs

ModalCommon UseExample
CanAbility, permission, possibilityShe can swim.
CouldPast ability, polite request, possibilityCould you help me?
MayPermission, possibilityYou may leave now.
MightPossibilityIt might rain.
MustNecessity, strong obligationYou must follow the rules.
ShouldAdvice, expectationYou should study regularly.
WillFuture, willingnessI will call you.
WouldPolite requests, preferences, hypothetical situationsWould you like some tea?
ShallSuggestions or offersShall we start?

Basic Structure

Subject + Modal + V1 + Object

He can speak English.
You should revise this chapter.
They must complete the work.

After a modal, use the base form (V1) of the main verb.

Uses of Important Modals

Can — Ability and Permission

I can drive.
Can I come in?

Could — Past Ability and Polite Requests

When I was young, I could run fast.
Could you open the window?

May — Permission and Possibility

May I enter the room?
She may arrive late.

Might — Possibility

We might visit Delhi tomorrow.

Must — Strong Necessity

You must wear a seat belt.

Should — Advice

You should drink enough water.

Will — Future and Willingness

I will help you.

Would — Polite Requests and Hypothetical Situations

Would you please wait?
I would travel more if I had enough time.

Negative Sentences

To make a negative sentence, put not after the modal.

She cannot swim.
You should not worry.
He must not enter.

Questions with Modals

Put the modal before the subject.

Can you help me?
Should I call him?
May I use your phone?

Common Mistakes

  • Do not use to after most modal verbs: “can go”, not “can to go”.
  • Use the base verb after a modal: “should study”, not “should studies”.
  • Do not add -s to the main verb after a modal.
  • Remember that must not expresses prohibition, not lack of necessity.
