Sentence Correction

Learn how to identify and correct common English grammar mistakes. Improve sentence accuracy with simple rules and practical examples.

What is Sentence Correction?

Sentence correction means finding and fixing grammatical errors in a sentence. The mistake may be related to tense, subject-verb agreement, articles, prepositions, pronouns, word usage or sentence structure.

Quick idea: First find the part of the sentence that sounds incorrect. Then identify the grammar rule involved and replace the incorrect form with the correct one.

Common Sentence Errors

Incorrect Correct Rule
He go to school every day. He goes to school every day. Subject-Verb Agreement
She have a car. She has a car. Subject-Verb Agreement
I am living here since 2020. I have been living here since 2020. Tense
He is good in English. He is good at English. Preposition
She is an university student. She is a university student. Article
Each students has a book. Each student has a book. Number / Agreement

How to Correct a Sentence

1. Read the complete sentence

Do not immediately change the first word that looks unusual. Read the complete sentence and understand its meaning first.

2. Find the subject and verb

Check whether the subject and verb agree in number and person.

The boys plays cricket. The boys play cricket.

3. Check the tense

Look for time expressions and make sure the verb form matches the intended time.

She went to school every day. She goes to school every day.

4. Check articles

Check whether a, an or the is required and whether the correct article has been used.

He is an European. He is a European.

5. Check prepositions

Some words are normally followed by particular prepositions.

She is interested on music. She is interested in music.

6. Check pronouns

Make sure the pronoun agrees correctly with the noun and is used in the correct form.

Me and Rahul went there. Rahul and I went there.

Important Areas to Check

  • Subject-Verb Agreement
  • Tenses
  • Articles
  • Prepositions
  • Pronouns
  • Adjectives and Adverbs
  • Conjunctions
  • Modals
  • Word order
  • Parallel structure

Sentence Correction in Exams

In competitive examinations, sentence correction questions often test one specific grammar rule. Instead of changing the whole sentence randomly, identify the exact error and apply the relevant rule.

Exam tip: Check the sentence systematically: Subject → Verb → Tense → Article → Preposition → Pronoun → Word order.

Practice Examples

He don't know the answer. He doesn't know the answer.
She is senior than me. She is senior to me.
I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday.
Neither of the boys are ready. Neither of the boys is ready.

Common Mistakes

  • Changing a correct part of the sentence unnecessarily.
  • Ignoring the subject-verb relationship.
  • Using the wrong tense with a clear time expression.
  • Choosing an article based only on spelling instead of pronunciation.
  • Translating prepositions directly from another language.
  • Focusing on vocabulary when the actual error is grammatical.
