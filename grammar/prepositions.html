ENGLISH GRAMMAR • PREPOSITIONS

Prepositions made simple.

Learn how to use common English prepositions with simple rules, clear examples and practical practice.

What is a Preposition?

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in a sentence. It can show place, time, direction, movement and other relationships.

Quick idea: Words such as in, on, at, by, for, since, under, between and among are common prepositions.

Common Prepositions at a Glance

Preposition Common Use Example
In Inside a place; months, years and longer periods. She lives in Delhi.
On Surface; days and dates. The book is on the table.
At Specific point or exact time. Meet me at 5 PM.
By Deadline or latest time; means of transport. Finish it by Monday.
For Duration of time. I have lived here for five years.
Since Starting point of an action or period. She has been here since Monday.
Between Position or relationship involving two people or things. The shop is between the bank and the school.
Among Relationship involving more than two people or things. He was sitting among his friends.

Prepositions of Time

In

Use in with months, years, seasons and longer periods.

I was born in 2000.
The exams will be held in March.

On

Use on with days and specific dates.

We will meet on Monday.
Her birthday is on 15 August.

At

Use at with specific clock times and certain fixed time expressions.

The train arrives at 6:30 PM.
I usually wake up at night.

Easy way to remember

In → longer period

On → day or date

At → exact time

Prepositions of Place

In

Use in when something is inside an area, container, city, country or enclosed space.

The children are in the room.
She lives in India.

On

Use on when something is touching or positioned on a surface.

The phone is on the table.

At

Use at for a particular point or location.

He is waiting at the bus stop.

For and Since

For — duration

For tells us how long something continues.

I have worked here for five years.
She has been studying for two hours.

Since — starting point

Since tells us when an action or situation began.

I have lived here since 2020.
She has been working since morning.

Other Important Prepositions

By and Until

By means no later than a particular time. Until means up to a particular time.

Submit the form by Friday.
I will wait until Friday.

Between and Among

Between is commonly used for a relationship involving two people or things, while among is commonly used when something is considered within a group.

The agreement was made between the two countries.
She was sitting among her classmates.

Under and Over

The shoes are under the bed.
The plane flew over the city.

Common Mistakes

✕ He arrived in 5 PM.
✓ He arrived at 5 PM.
✕ I was born on 2000.
✓ I was born in 2000.
✕ She has lived here since five years.
✓ She has lived here for five years.
✕ The book is in the table.
✓ The book is on the table.

How to Learn Prepositions

Prepositions can be difficult because one preposition may have different uses. Instead of memorizing isolated words, learn them through common expressions and complete sentences.

  • Learn in, on and at together.
  • Practice time and place separately.
  • Remember for for duration and since for a starting point.
  • Learn common verb + preposition combinations.
  • Practice with real sentences instead of only memorizing lists.
