Subject-Verb Agreement

Learn how subjects and verbs agree in number, with simple rules, examples and common exam mistakes.

What is Subject-Verb Agreement?

Subject-verb agreement means that the verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

Quick idea: A singular subject generally takes a singular verb, while a plural subject generally takes a plural verb.

Basic Rule

He plays cricket.   ✓
They play cricket.   ✓

In the present simple tense, remember that he, she and it generally take a verb ending in -s/-es, while I, you, we and they use the base form.

Important Rules

1. Singular Subject + Singular Verb

The boy runs fast.
My sister likes music.

2. Plural Subject + Plural Verb

The boys run fast.
My sisters like music.

3. Two Subjects Joined by “and”

Two subjects joined by and generally take a plural verb.

Riya and Priya are friends.
Tom and John play football.

4. Subjects Joined by “or” or “nor”

When subjects are joined by or, nor, either...or or neither...nor, the verb generally agrees with the subject closer to it.

Either the teacher or the students are responsible.
Either the students or the teacher is responsible.

5. Indefinite Pronouns

Words such as everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, each and either are generally treated as singular.

Everyone is ready.
Each of the students has a book.

6. “Each” and “Every”

Each and every normally take a singular noun and singular verb.

Every student has a notebook.
Each player is ready.

7. Collective Nouns

A collective noun can take a singular verb when the group is considered as one unit.

The team is winning.
The committee has reached a decision.

8. Words Between Subject and Verb

Do not let a phrase between the subject and verb confuse you. Find the real subject first.

The box of old books is heavy.

The subject is box, not books.

9. “There is” and “There are”

The verb agrees with the noun that follows it.

There is a book on the table.
There are three books on the table.

10. Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns such as information, advice, furniture and knowledge generally take singular verbs.

The information is useful.
The furniture is expensive.

Quick Reference

SubjectVerbExample
He / She / ItSingular verbShe works hard.
I / You / We / TheyBase/plural formThey work hard.

There + nounAgrees with following nounThere are two books.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing the verb according to a nearby noun instead of the real subject.
  • Using a plural verb after each or every.
  • Forgetting the -s/-es with a third-person singular subject in the present simple.
  • Using there is with a plural noun.
